## Summary points

- The paper studies scaling behavior specific to mixture-of-experts diffusion language models, using controlled sweeps over models from roughly 60M to 7.5B parameters and compute budgets from 10^17 to 10^20 FLOPs.
- Its experiments indicate that, relative to reported autoregressive-model trends, optimal nominal batch size increases faster with compute, optimal learning rate decreases faster, and compute allocation is nearly balanced but slightly tilted toward additional training data.
- The architecture sweeps report that larger scales favor lower activation ratios and larger expert pools, while moderate expert granularity (G=8–16) and a 33.3% shared-expert capacity ratio remain effective; these findings guide a 30B-total/3B-activated model trained on 23.5T tokens.

## Main takeaway

The preprint presents empirical scaling guidance for MoE diffusion language models and reports that LLaDA MoE v2 can approach comparable autoregressive models on several tasks with fewer pretraining tokens, though the benchmark claims are author-reported and some comparisons involve different post-training procedures.
