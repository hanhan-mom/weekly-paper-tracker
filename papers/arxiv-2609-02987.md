# Tail-Likelihood Reinforcement Learning

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02987
First opened: 2026-09-24

AI-generated from selected main-body sections; verify against the full paper.

## Summary points

- The paper introduces Tail-Likelihood Reinforcement Learning (TailRL), which optimizes the average log-probability that a rollout exceeds each reward threshold rather than optimizing only expected reward; for binary rewards, it reduces to MaxRL.
- The authors show theoretically that TailRL’s gradient is a harmonic mixture of Best-of-k gradients and derive a critic-free, finite-rollout estimator that can be used by changing the advantage calculation in an existing policy-gradient pipeline.
- Across object localization, maze navigation, GUI grounding, and code optimization, the authors report that TailRL better preserves rare high-reward behaviors and improves inference-time sampling; in the detailed ImageNet experiment, larger rollout groups increasingly align with the population gradient, and TailRL outperforms matched-rollout GRPO and RLOO on CorLoc, mean IoU, and Best-of-1024 IoU.

## Main takeaway

TailRL reframes continuous-reward optimization around the likelihood of exceeding all reward thresholds, with theory and experiments suggesting stronger retention of rare high-quality outcomes and Best-of-k scaling, though the reported benefits are most relevant when such outcomes remain reachable and depend on larger rollout budgets to approximate the population objective.
