# Hallucination is a Consequence of Space-Optimality: A Rate-Distortion Theorem for Membership Testing

First opened: 2026-09-21

## Summary points

- The paper models factual judgment as membership testing over a finite universe and derives a sparse-regime rate–distortion bound: storing n facts at specified error levels requires and can achieve approximately n times the minimum KL divergence between fact and non-fact score distributions.
- Under logarithmic loss and limited memory, the claimed optimum retains high confidence on facts while assigning the same high confidence to a subset of non-facts, making confident false positives more efficient than uniform uncertainty or systematic forgetting; eliminating them instead raises false negatives or memory cost.
- Synthetic-transformer and LoRA experiments on random IDs and ISBNs show the predicted high-confidence non-fact tail; empirical KL was reported within 14–22% of the lower bound, with agreement strongest when facts were sparse.

## Main takeaway

In the paper's finite, random-fact membership-testing model, confident hallucinations arise as a space-efficient error pattern, so reducing false positives without more memory generally increases forgetting or over-refusal.
