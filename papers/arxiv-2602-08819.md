# Bayesian Preference Learning for Test-Time Steerable Reward Models

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08819
First opened: 2026-09-25

## Summary points

- The paper presents Variational In-Context Reward Modeling (ICRM), which represents pairwise Bradley–Terry preferences as Beta posteriors whose mean and concentration are inferred from test-time preference demonstrations; a KL term toward a uniform Beta prior is intended to temper overconfident reward maximization.
- Experiments with Qwen3-4B and Llama-3.2-3B report that additional demonstrations improve directional and calibrated steering: average RM-Bench accuracy rises from 60.5% with one demonstration to 70.8% with 64, and ICRM shows lower calibration error than the tested Qwen3-4B judge baseline.
- The model can trade off conflicting objectives and reportedly improves reinforcement learning on MATH-500, but its flexibility also lets demonstrations steer it toward harmful or dishonest responses, and the evidence is limited to the selected models, datasets, and baselines.

## Main takeaway

ICRM offers a Bayesian method for adapting a classifier reward model to new single- or multi-objective preferences at test time, with promising reported gains but limited evaluation and an important risk that malicious demonstrations can deliberately reverse safety preferences.
