## Summary points

- The model treats customer urgency as private information and lets a capacity-constrained monopolist choose both a dynamic information process and its pricing menu.
- Its optimum uses one common "greedy exploration" process for every customer, while screening urgency only through time caps: impatient customers choose smaller, higher-priced-per-unit caps and patient customers choose larger, discounted caps.
- Within the model, degrading the service for cheaper tiers reduces revenue; varying access limits instead explains common-service quotas, latency tiers, and volume discounts.

## Main takeaway

For capacity-constrained dynamic information services, the paper argues that providers should screen customers by capped access to one customer-preferred process rather than by degrading service quality.
